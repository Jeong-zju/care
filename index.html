Overview

The best action depends on what the teammate will do next.

Consider two robots carrying an object. One of them has to decide when to turn. Turning now shortens its own path, but it can destabilize the object if the partner is still regrasping. Waiting briefly makes little direct progress, yet it lets the partner finish the grasp so the team can proceed. The uncertainty that matters is what the team will do in response to the next action. The same decision arises when releasing an object in a handoff, yielding a shared workspace, or synchronizing a lift.

We call a robot's prediction of this action-dependent effect a team-response belief. For each candidate action, it describes the likely gain or loss in team utility that comes from letting teammates adapt, compared with teammates that keep their nominal behavior. It is conditioned on the candidate action, inferred from the robot's own history, and keeps its uncertainty. It needs no semantic label such as "the partner is ready" and no reconstruction of the partner's hidden intent.

Ordinary demonstrations provide almost no supervision for this belief. A successful trajectory shows the action that was taken and the response that followed, but nothing about how teammates would have responded to the alternatives. CARE (Cooperative Action–Response Belief Estimation) makes the response contribution an explicit training target through paired interaction augmentation, learns a predictive belief encoder that conditions nominal actions, then trains a candidate-conditioned response readout with the encoder and action generator frozen. Calibrated selection chooses an alternative or retains the nominal action at execution.

Three contributions

  1. A definition and a target. The team-response belief, with a concrete supervisory signal from matched reactive and replay continuations.
  2. An analysis. When response overcomes a candidate's direct disadvantage, and how source and estimation error bound the recoverable gain under conservative selection.
  3. A local evaluator and a protocol. Candidate selection from local history without rollouts, evaluated so that response supervision is separated from proposal quality, total-value learning, and access to the augmented outcomes.

Same start, two choices

Judged by its own motion, the default action is not worse. It becomes worse once the teammate's reaction counts.

Method

Predict what the response contributes, then act on it with a calibrated bound.

  1. 01

    Propose

    A predictive belief encoder learns the interaction from local history and conditions the nominal action u0. The encoder and action generator are then frozen. K = 6 candidates include this nominal chunk and local behavioral alternatives such as waiting, retreating, and slowing down.

  2. 02

    Label with paired interaction augmentation

    From a common state, each candidate is compared with the nominal action under two teammate behaviors: reactive teammates keep running their policies, replay teammates re-issue the commands recorded under the nominal action. The reactive contrast is the total advantage Ak, the replay contrast is the direct advantage Dk, and their difference Rk is the response label. The demonstrations stay in the training set.

  3. 03

    Select with a calibrated lower bound

    A candidate-conditioned response readout uses the frozen predictive representation to predict quantiles of D, R and A plus an adverse-event probability for every candidate. Conformal calibration on held-out families turns the lower quantile of A into a lower bound. The best feasible alternative runs only if its bound clears the margin; otherwise the robot keeps u0. Execution uses local history only, with no rollouts.

One paired contrast, three labels

Utility U is a fixed weighted sum of progress, success, time to success and coordination penalties over a 16-step horizon. Collisions and drops are supervised separately as adverse events.

Identifiability

Paired supervision identifies the belief.

Two teammate behaviors can agree on every observed nominal continuation and still respond differently to an untried action, so nominal data alone cannot tell them apart. Matched reactive and replay branches turn Rk into draws from the belief, and the pinball loss recovers its conditional quantiles.

Decision value

Response matters when it flips the best action.

Response information has decision value Γ(H) exactly when some candidate's relative response gain exceeds its direct disadvantage. A mean-based belief choice recovers at least Γ − 2e, where e sums the source-fidelity error and the estimation errors of the direct and response means.

Calibrated intervention

Overrides come with coverage.

With exchangeable calibration and test families, the calibrated lower bound covers every candidate's realized advantage with probability at least 1 − α. A released alternative has paired utility above the margin δ on that coverage event. The guarantee concerns the paired reference distribution: source fidelity, exchangeability, and teammates departing from the reference policy at execution can affect deployment.

Results

Strong where the preferred action depends on the partner's timing, and honest about where it does not.

On RoboFactory, CARE achieves the highest average success among the evaluated policies. Most of the gain comes from Take Photo and Pass Shoe, where one robot must hold a pose or an object until its partner is in position and where maintaining support and waiting for a partner are central to completion. CARE falls behind on Place Food, Camera Alignment and Long Pipeline Delivery, where precise positioning or a long sequential chain dominates and there is less for the interaction contrast to change. On MARS, absolute performance is low for all policies; most of CARE's margin comes from Strike Cube Hard, whose success depends on a single well-timed joint impact, and CARE is the only policy with any successes on Four Robots Stack Cube.

Scope and limitations

Team-response beliefs depend on what the local history reveals about the partner, on which actions are available and on how team utility is measured. Weak proposals leave little gain to recover, and a short horizon can miss delayed reactions. The paired contrast depends on the nominal trace and may put replayed teammates in states that never occur in ordinary interaction, so augmentation bias, unseen partners and policy-induced distribution shift can affect both belief accuracy and calibration. CARE already conditions nominal actions on predictive beliefs. Future work can adapt proposal distributions, extend response learning to changing teammate policies, and combine selection with runtime monitoring.
